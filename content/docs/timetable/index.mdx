The **Timetable** tools build your school's weekly class schedule for you. You describe what's
true about your school — who teaches what, when teachers and rooms are available, and how many
lessons each class needs — and the **generator** builds a timetable that follows your rules. It
places every lesson it can and tells you why any remaining lesson couldn't be placed. You then
review it, adjust anything by hand, and **activate** a [timetable group](./timetable-groups) so
teachers, students, and parents follow the live week and **class sessions** appear with real dates.

<Callout title="Who this is for">
Academic planners and school administrators who build the school schedule.
</Callout>

## The big picture

Building a timetable has three kinds of work:

1. **Set up the inputs** — the subjects, classes, teachers, and rooms the schedule is built
   from, plus the [school schedule](../settings/school-schedule), each group's
   [timetable config](./timetable-config), and each subject's teaching load.
2. **Describe reality and set the rules** — when teachers, rooms, and classes are available, how
   many hours a teacher can work, and the preferences the generator should try to satisfy.
3. **Generate, review, and activate** — run the generator on a group, check the result, fix
   anything by hand, and activate so dated sessions are created.

## Two kinds of setup: reality vs. rules

It helps to keep two ideas apart, because they live in two different places:

- **Reality (Config tabs)** — facts the timetable must respect: a teacher is only available in
  the mornings, a teacher works at most six hours a day, a class only comes in the morning, Maths
  needs four lessons a week.
- **Rules (Solver policy)** — preferences that make a timetable *good* rather than just valid:
  avoid gaps in a teacher's day, keep a class in the same room, keep two demanding subjects apart.
  You turn each one on or off and decide how much it matters. **Solver policy** is opened from a
  **timetable group**, not from Config.

## The order to work in

<Steps>

<Step>
### Check the prerequisites

Make sure subjects, classes, terms, teachers, rooms, and teacher assignments all exist. See
[Prerequisites](./prerequisites).
</Step>

<Step>
### Set the school schedule

Define the school's default week in Settings. See [School schedule](../settings/school-schedule).
</Step>

<Step>
### Set the timetable config

Define (or narrow) the weekly grid of days, hours, and breaks for the levels you will schedule.
See [Timetable config](./timetable-config).
</Step>

<Step>
### Set the teaching load

For each subject at a level, say how many lessons per week it needs and how they are shaped. See
[Teaching load](./teaching-load).
</Step>

<Step>
### Confirm the lessons to schedule

Make sure each class–subject is assigned to a teacher so the generator knows what to place. See
[Lessons and assignments](./lessons-and-assignments).
</Step>

<Step>
### Describe reality

Set when teachers and rooms are unavailable, teacher workload limits, homerooms, and class hours.
See [Scheduling setup](./scheduling-setup).
</Step>

<Step>
### Create a group and choose your rules

Create a [timetable group](./timetable-groups) and adjust its **Solver policy**. See
[Constraints](./constraints).
</Step>

<Step>
### Generate and activate

Run the generator from the group, review the result, make manual edits, and activate. See
[Generate and activate](./generate-and-publish) and
[Activate and class sessions](./activate-and-sessions).
</Step>

</Steps>

## Before you start

- An **academic year** must be created and selected.
- The **academic foundation** should already be in place — levels, classes, subjects, terms,
  rooms, and teachers. See
  [Build the academic foundation](../getting-started/build-the-academic-foundation).
- Set the **[School schedule](../settings/school-schedule)** early.

<Callout title="Pick the right year first">
The Timetable pages show the academic year selected in the year switcher. Select the year you are
building the schedule for before you begin.
</Callout>
