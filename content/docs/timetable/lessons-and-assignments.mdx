The generator places **lessons**. This article explains what a lesson is, where the number of
lessons comes from, and how you tell the app who teaches each one. Getting this right is what
makes a generated timetable correct.

<Callout title="Who this is for">
Administrators and academic planners preparing what the timetable will schedule.
</Callout>

## What gets scheduled

A **lesson** is one weekly meeting of a subject for a class — for example *Class 6A, Mathematics*.
When the generator runs, it gives each lesson:

- a **day** and a **period**,
- a **room**, and
- the **teacher** assigned to it.

A class usually has several lessons of each subject per week, and the generator places them all
without clashes.

## How many lessons each subject has

The number of weekly lessons comes from **[Teaching load](./teaching-load)** for each subject at a
level. There you say how many **meetings** the subject has per week, and whether any are
**double** (or longer) — several **periods** in a row.

A **period** is one slot on the day grid. A **lesson** is one meeting to place. For example,
*Physics* might be **two singles and one double**: three lessons, but four periods of teaching
time. The generator places three Physics meetings for each class in that level and keeps the double
as two consecutive periods.

See [Teaching load](./teaching-load) for the full glossary (period, lesson, lesson block, Split,
room type) and how to fill the editor.

<Callout title="Where to set this">
Set teaching load under **Academic → Timetable → Config → Teaching load**. Do this before you
generate so the generator knows how much of each subject to place.
</Callout>

## Assign teachers to classes

This is the key input. For each class and each subject, you assign the **teacher responsible**
for it. The generator schedules exactly these assignments — a class–subject with no teacher can't
be placed.

You can assign teachers in two places:

- **Academic → Teacher Assignments → Class Assignments** — click a cell and pick the teacher. Best
  for filling a whole term quickly. See [Teacher Assignments](./teacher-assignments).
- **A teacher's profile → Classes** — add assignments for that one teacher, as below.

<Steps>

<Step>
### Open the teacher

Open the teacher's profile, go to the **Classes** tab, and click **Add Class Assignment**.
</Step>

<Step>
### Choose what the teacher teaches

| Field | Required | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Level** | Yes | The level of the class. |
| **Class** | Yes | The class they teach. |
| **Term** | No | One term, or **All Terms**. |
| **Subjects - Active Teacher Assignments** | Yes | One or more subjects they teach to that class. Only subjects the teacher is eligible for at this level are offered — set them in **Subject Eligibility** first. |

Click **Submit**.
</Step>

<Step>
### Repeat until every class–subject is covered

The **Class Assignments** grid shows what is still **Unassigned**. Generating also lists every
class–subject that still has no teacher. See [Generate and activate](./generate-and-publish).
</Step>

</Steps>

## Split a class into groups (optional)

Some subjects are taught to **part of a class at a time** — for example half the class does the
science lab while the other half does something else. To handle this:

1. Open the class (**Academic → Classes**) and create its groups on the **Class groups** tab.
2. In [Teaching load](./teaching-load), turn on **Split** for the lesson blocks taught in groups.
3. If two groups should have their lessons at the same time, link them with **Same slot** (below),
   or use **Run two subjects in parallel** in Solver policy to do it for many classes at once.

## Link related lessons

Sometimes two lessons need to be placed in relation to each other. Open the group's **Solver
policy** → **Lessons** tab and click **Add lesson link**:

| Link | What it means | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Same slot** | Two lessons run at the exact same time, for different groups of a class. | The two halves of a divided class have their parallel lessons together. |
| **Back-to-back** | Two lessons run in consecutive periods. | A lab right after the lecture. |
| **Same day** | Two lessons fall on the same day. | Keeping a paired theory and practical on one day. |
| **Same half-day** | Two lessons fall in the same morning or afternoon. | Grouping related lessons close together. |

**Same slot** links are always kept. The other three follow the **Lesson sequence** rule: you
switch it on or off and set its importance once, for all of them. See [Constraints](./constraints).

## Common use cases

- **Standard class** — assign one teacher per subject; the generator does the rest.
- **Lab subject** — set the lab lesson as a double so it gets two periods together.
- **Split language groups** — create class groups, turn on **Split** in the teaching load, and
  link the groups' lessons with **Same slot**.
