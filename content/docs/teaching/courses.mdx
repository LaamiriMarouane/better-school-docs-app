A **course** is the curriculum for one **subject at one level**, for a term. It's organised into
**chapters**, and each chapter holds **lessons**. You build a course once, and every class at that
level follows it — so the structure stays consistent, and you can reuse it next year.

<Callout title="Who this is for">
Administrators and teachers who plan what's taught in each subject.
</Callout>

## How a course is organised

```
Course  (a subject, at a level, for a term)
└─ Chapter
   └─ Lesson
      └─ Lesson content  (written by the teacher — see Lessons)
```

A course belongs to a specific **subject + level + term**, and there's one course per that
combination. The chapters and lessons give every class the same backbone; teachers then add their
own [lesson content](./lessons) on top.

## Before you start

- The subject must be **assigned to the level** (a level subject), and **terms** must exist. See
  [Build the academic foundation](../getting-started/build-the-academic-foundation).

## Create a course

<Steps>

<Step>
### Open the curriculum

Go to **Courses** (or **Curriculum**) and create a new course.

<Screenshot
  label="Courses list"
  caption="Each course is a subject at a level, organised into chapters and lessons."
/>
</Step>

<Step>
### Fill in the course details

| Field | Required | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Title** | Yes | The course name. |
| **Description** | No | A short summary of what the course covers. |
| **Subject, level, and term** | Yes | Which subject, at which level, for which term this course belongs to. |
</Step>

<Step>
### Add chapters and lessons

Add **chapters** in the order they're taught, then add **lessons** within each chapter. You can
reorder both at any time. Each lesson has its own details — type, duration, objectives — covered in
[Lessons](./lessons).
</Step>

<Step>
### Publish the course

A course starts as a **draft**. **Publish** it when the structure is ready, so it becomes available
to the classes at that level.
</Step>

</Steps>

## Who manages a course's curriculum

The **curriculum** — the course's chapters and lessons, and publishing it — is managed by teachers
given **curriculum ownership** of that subject, level, and term. An administrator grants ownership,
and can **assign many teachers across many subjects at once**. There are two roles:

| Role | What they can do with the curriculum |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary** | Full control — shape the chapters and lessons, **publish**, and delete the course. |
| **Contributor** | Shape the chapters and lessons, but **can't** publish or delete. |

<Callout title="Curriculum ownership is not the same as lesson content">
These roles control the **curriculum structure** and publishing. They're **separate** from the
**lesson content** a teacher writes for their own class — which is governed by *teaching* the
subject, not by owning the curriculum. See [Lessons](./lessons).
</Callout>

## Sharing a course across classes

A course is shared by **all the classes at its level**. Once published, its lessons are available to
those classes. Each class's teacher then writes the [lesson content](./lessons) and decides what
their students can see — so two classes can follow the same course while each teacher keeps their
own materials.

## Reuse from year to year

When you start a new academic year, a course can be **carried over** so you don't rebuild the
curriculum from scratch — the chapters and lessons come across, ready to adjust. This is part of
copying an academic year forward.

## Common use cases

- **New subject curriculum** — create the course, lay out chapters and lessons, and publish.
- **Same course, several classes** — build it once at the level; every class follows it.
- **Next year** — carry the course forward and tweak rather than starting over.
