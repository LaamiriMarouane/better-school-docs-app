The **gradebook** is where every mark a student earns comes together. Results from all assessment
types — scheduled [exams](./exams), [assignments](./assignments), [quizzes](./quizzes), and
manually entered grades — are placed in **grade categories**, averaged within **marking periods**,
and combined — according to the level's **grading scheme** — into each student's subject grade
and overall year average.

<Callout title="Who this is for">
Teachers and administrators managing grades, and students and parents viewing them.
</Callout>

## Where grades come from

A student's subject grade is built from every piece of assessed work recorded during the year.
There are three kinds of assessment, each with its own workflow:

**Exams** — scheduled sittings that go through a template, timetable slot, room, supervisors, and
separate exam attendance. Results stay private until published. See [Exams](./exams).

**Assignments and quizzes** — work given to students to complete. File-based assignments
(homework, projects) collect submissions; quizzes are auto-graded. See
[Assignments](./assignments) and [Quizzes](./quizzes).

**Free assessments** — manually entered grade columns with no submission workflow. Used for
things like oral presentations, class participation, behavior, notebook checks, or bonus points.
Teachers open the subject's gradebook and enter scores directly.

Every assessment belongs to a **grade category** and is recorded inside a **marking period**.

## Marking periods

A **marking period** is the grading unit within a term. Every term gets at least one marking
period automatically — when there is only one, it covers the whole term at 100% weight. You can
split a term into more periods when your school structure requires it, for example Midterm and
End-of-term, or four quarterly periods across two semesters.

<Callout title="One period per term is the default">
Most schools start with one marking period per term. You only need to add more when you want
sub-term grading divisions such as quarterly assessments or midterm grades.
</Callout>

Assessments are always recorded inside a marking period — never directly at the term level. When
there is only one period, the experience is identical to a term-based gradebook.

## Grade categories

A **grade category** groups assessments of a kind — for example *Exams*, *Assignments*, *Quizzes*
— and gives each group a **weight**. Within a marking period, all category weights for a subject
must add up to 100%. You can:

- set how much each category **counts** toward the period grade (its weight),
- mark a category as **not counting**, when it is for information only,
- define categories for the whole school or per **level**.

## Grading scheme

Each level has a **grading scheme**: an ordered list of entries whose weights sum to 100% and
that together make up the subject year grade. An entry is either:

- an **internal term** — the term's contribution, itself a weighted sum of its marking periods,
- an **external exam** — a national or regional exam that covers a subset of subjects with its own
  scoring and coefficients.

| Example — two equal terms | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Term 1 | 50% |
| Term 2 | 50% |

| Example — terms + regional exam (Grade 6) | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| Term 1 | 25% |
| Term 2 | 25% |
| Regional exam | 50% |

You can load a **preset** (standard two-term, Grade 6, Grade 9, BAC) or build the scheme from
scratch. Editing the scheme never affects past years — each year's weights are snapshotted
independently.

## How a grade is worked out

The calculation runs in three nested steps.

**Step 1 — Period grade (the leaf)**

Within a marking period: assessments are averaged inside their category, then the category
averages are combined by their weights → **period grade**. This uses the level's grading scale,
calculation method, and rounding.

**Step 2 — Term grade**

If a term has multiple marking periods, their period grades are combined by each period's
within-term weight → **term grade**. When there is only one period, the term grade equals the
period grade.

**Step 3 — Subject year grade**

The scheme entries (internal terms and any external exam) are combined by their year-ring weights
→ **subject year grade**.

**Moyenne générale (overall average)**

Each subject's year grade is multiplied by its **coefficient** (the school-wide weighting for
that subject at that level), summed across all subjects, then divided by the total coefficients →
the student's overall average.

Grades use your school's **grading scale** and **rounding** — for example marks out of 20,
rounded to one decimal place — configured per level in the gradebook settings.

## External exams

An **external exam** (regional or national) is a separate grading scheme entry. It covers a
specific subset of subjects with its own coefficients and maximum score. Those coefficients are
used only to compute the **exam average**, which appears on the report card as a separate line.

**The exam average is independent of the overall average.** The student's overall average always
uses the school's own coefficients across all subjects — the exam's coefficients never feed into
it.

When a subject is not covered by the external exam, the exam's weight is dropped for that subject
and the remaining entries are renormalised so they still sum to 100%.

## Calculation scenarios

### Scenario 1 — Two equal terms, no external exam

A standard primary-level setup: two equal terms, one marking period each.

**Scheme:** Term 1 = 50%, Term 2 = 50%

Student, Maths: T1 = 14, T2 = 12.

| Part | Grade | Weight | Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Term 1 | 14 | 50% | 7.0 |
| Term 2 | 12 | 50% | 6.0 |
| **Year grade** | | | **13.0** |

### Scenario 2 — Two terms + regional exam (Grade 6)

**Scheme:** Term 1 = 25%, Term 2 = 25%, Regional = 50%

**Maths** — covered by the regional exam:

| Part | Grade | Weight | Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Term 1 | 14 | 25% | 3.5 |
| Term 2 | 12 | 25% | 3.0 |
| Regional | 16 | 50% | 8.0 |
| **Year grade** | | | **14.5** |

**Physical Education** — not covered by the regional exam. The exam weight is dropped and Term 1 /
Term 2 are renormalised to 50% each:

| Part | Grade | Weight (renormalised) | Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Term 1 | 15 | 50% | 7.5 |
| Term 2 | 13 | 50% | 6.5 |
| **Year grade** | | | **14.0** |

### Scenario 3 — Multiple marking periods per term (quarter system)

**Scheme:** Semester 1 = 45%, Semester 2 = 45%, Final exam = 10%
**Within Semester 1:** Quarter 1 = 50%, Quarter 2 = 50%
**Within Semester 2:** Quarter 3 = 50%, Quarter 4 = 50%

Student, Maths:

| Marking period | Grade | Within-term weight | Term grade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Q1 | 15 | 50% | — |
| Q2 | 13 | 50% | **14.0** (S1) |
| Q3 | 16 | 50% | — |
| Q4 | 14 | 50% | **15.0** (S2) |

Year grade = 14.0 × 45% + 15.0 × 45% + Final (12) × 10% = 6.3 + 6.75 + 1.2 = **14.25**

## Grade statuses

In the gradebook grid, each cell shows where a student stands:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Graded** | A mark has been recorded. |
| **Pending** | The work isn't marked yet. |
| **Absent** | The student was absent for the assessment. |
| **Exempt** | The student is excused from it. |

## The gradebook views

- **By term** — term → marking periods → assessments; edit a score and the grades recalculate
  live up the chain.
- **By class** — each subject's final year grade, overall average, class rank, and mention.
- **All periods** — every period value and final grade per subject; open a final to see the full
  breakdown: which entries counted, which were dropped and renormalised, any pass-fail gate flags.
- **Student view** — what a student (and their parents) see: grade per subject, breakdown by
  category and period, class rank.

## Adjust a grade

- **Override** — set a student's period grade by hand, with a reason, when the calculated grade
  shouldn't apply. Term and year grades are always computed from period values and cannot be
  overridden directly.
- **Remark** — add a note about a student's performance without changing the grade.

## Report cards

You can produce a student's **report card** — period grades, term grades, year grade, overall
average, rank, and mention — ready to share with families. When the level has an external exam,
the report card also shows the **exam average** as a separate line.

Grades on a report card are snapshotted at generation, so they stay fixed even if assessment
marks are edited afterwards.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a marking period?

A marking period is the grading unit within a term. Every term starts with one marking period at
100% of the term weight — you see it listed even if you never split it. Assessments are recorded
and averaged inside a marking period, not directly at the term level.

### How is a student's year grade calculated?

The year grade for a subject is a weighted sum of the level's grading scheme entries. Each
internal term contributes its term grade (itself a weighted average of its marking periods), and
any external exam contributes its score for that subject. Weights are set in the scheme and must
sum to 100%.

### What happens when a subject isn't covered by an external exam?

The exam entry's weight is dropped for that subject and the remaining entries are renormalised so
they still sum to 100%. The year grade is computed from terms only, with a visible note that the
exam didn't apply to the subject.

### Can different levels have different term weights?

Yes. Each level has its own grading scheme. A primary level might use two equal terms (50%/50%),
while Grade 6 might use Term 1 (25%), Term 2 (25%), and a regional exam (50%). Changes to one
level's scheme never affect other levels.

### What is the moyenne générale?

The **moyenne générale** (overall average) is the student's weighted average across all subjects,
using each subject's school coefficient. It is always computed from school coefficients over all
subjects. The external exam's own coefficients are only used for the separate exam average line —
they never feed into the overall average.

### Can I split a term into multiple marking periods?

Yes. In the level's grading setup, open the term and add marking periods — for example Midterm
and End-of-term, or four quarterly periods. Set each period's within-term weight so they sum to
100%. Teachers then record assessments inside a specific period, not at the term level.
