The **gradebook** is where every mark a student earns comes together. Results from
[exams](./exams), [assignments](./assignments), [quizzes](./quizzes) and free assessments appear in
one grid per class, and the averages update as you enter marks.

<Callout title="Who this is for">
Teachers and administrators managing grades, and students and parents viewing them.
</Callout>

## Open the gradebook

Go to **Assessments → Gradebook**, then choose:

- **Select class**: the class to grade. Teachers see the classes they teach.
- the term: one term, or **All** to see every term side by side. The current term is marked
  **(Current)**.
- the subject: one subject, or **All subjects**.

## Read the grid

Each row is a student. Columns are grouped by term, then by subject. Inside a subject, marks are
sorted into folders:

- **Exams**: scheduled exams. See [Exams](./exams).
- **Assignments**: file assignments and quizzes, graded from their own pages. See
  [Assignments](./assignments) and [Quizzes](./quizzes).
- **Free assessments**: marks you enter directly, such as oral work, participation or a notebook
  check.

When a term is split into marking periods, each period has its own columns and an **Avg** column.
The **Term Avg** column always comes last. The bottom row shows the class mean for each column.

Above the grid you can:

- turn on **Heatmap** or **Mentions** to colour cells by result or show the mention instead of the
  number,
- turn **Score colors** on or off,
- **Collapse all** / **Expand all** subjects, or collapse one subject to see only its term average.

A column marked with a crossed-out eye is **not published**: its marks are visible to staff but
don't count in any average until the results are published.

Marks that don't count toward the average are greyed out. Absent and exempt marks show as **ABS**
and **EX**; whether each one is dropped from the average or counts as zero is set in
[Grading setup → Year policy](./grading-setup#year-policy) under **Exception codes**.

## Enter grades

1. Tap the column header and choose **Edit grades**.
2. Type each student's mark. Use the toolbar to **Fill empty**, **Set all**, or mark selected
   students **Absent** or **Exempt**. **Undo** and **Redo** are available while you edit.
3. Tap **Save grades**.

For an exam, the header menu first asks you to **Mark completed** before grades can be entered.
Use **Publish Results** when the marks are ready; until then, students don't see them and they
don't count in averages. **Unpublish Results** hides them again.

Assignment and quiz columns follow their source: grade and publish them from the assignment.
For subjects that use competency grading, the header menu also offers **Grade competencies**. See
[Competency-based grading](./competencies).

## Add a free assessment

1. In the subject's header, tap **Add assessment**.
2. Fill in **Name**, **Type** (participation, oral, notebook check, project…), **Category**,
   **Max Score** and **Date**.
3. Leave **Counts toward average** on if the marks should count. A counting assessment needs a
   category.
4. Save, then enter the marks with **Edit grades**.

A new free assessment starts unpublished. When the marks are final, open its header menu and
choose **Publish Results**. Only then do students see the marks and do they count in averages.

## Write remarks

Remarks are written on the **Term Avg** column:

- **One student**: tap the remark icon in the student's **Term Avg** cell.
- **Whole class**: tap the **Term Avg** header and choose **Edit remarks**. Type a remark per
  student, or use **Fill empty** and **Set all**, then **Save remarks**.

While writing, you can insert a ready-made phrase from the [comment bank](./grading-setup#comment-bank).
Remarks appear on the student's report card.

## Override an average

When a calculated average shouldn't apply, you can replace it by hand:

1. Tap the student's **Avg** cell for the marking period. If the term isn't split, tap the
   **Term Avg** cell.
2. In **Edit term average**, enter the **Override value** and, optionally, a **Reason**. The
   calculated value is shown for comparison.
3. Save. To go back to the calculated value, open the cell again and choose **Remove override**.

Overrides are only possible when **Allow manual average override** is on in
[Grading setup → Year policy](./grading-setup#year-policy).

## When grading is closed

Each term has a **Grading Status** (**Open**, **Locked**, **Finalized**) and an optional **Lock Date**.
Administrators set them with **Update Grading Settings** on the term, in the academic year's list of
terms.

| Action | Open | Locked | Finalized or Lock Date passed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enter marks in counting columns, add assessments | Yes | No | No |
| Override an average | Yes | Administrators only | No |
| Write remarks | Yes | No | No |

Columns that don't count toward the average can still be edited after a period closes.

## How a grade is worked out

1. **Period grade**: inside a marking period, marks are averaged within each grade category, then
   the categories are combined by the weights set for that subject in that term.
2. **Term grade**: if a term has several marking periods, their grades are combined by each
   period's weight. With one period, the term grade is the period grade.
3. **Year grade**: the terms (and any external exam) are combined by the weights in the level's
   grading scheme.
4. **Overall average**: each subject's year grade is multiplied by the subject's coefficient, then
   divided by the total of the coefficients.

Weights and coefficients are set in [Grading setup](./grading-setup). Grades use the school's
display scale and rounding, for example marks out of 20 rounded to one decimal.

### Example: two equal terms

Scheme: Term 1 = 50%, Term 2 = 50%. A student has 14 in Term 1 and 12 in Term 2 in Maths.

| Part | Grade | Weight | Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Term 1 | 14 | 50% | 7.0 |
| Term 2 | 12 | 50% | 6.0 |
| **Year grade** | | | **13.0** |

### Example: two terms and a regional exam

Scheme: Term 1 = 25%, Term 2 = 25%, Regional exam = 50%.

| Part | Grade | Weight | Contribution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Term 1 | 14 | 25% | 3.5 |
| Term 2 | 12 | 25% | 3.0 |
| Regional exam | 16 | 50% | 8.0 |
| **Year grade** | | | **14.5** |

A subject that isn't part of the regional exam is graded on its terms only, in the same
proportions. In this example, Term 1 and Term 2 then count for half each.

## External exams

An external exam (regional or national) covers some subjects, with its own coefficients. Those
coefficients are only used for the **exam average**, shown as a separate line on the report card.
The student's overall average always uses the school's own coefficients across all subjects.

## What students and parents see

Students and parents open **Grades** to see each subject's average, the marks behind it and the
student's rank. Exam results and free assessments appear once they are published. Published
report cards can be downloaded from the same screen.

## Report cards

Administrators generate report cards from the **Report cards** button above the grid. See
[Report cards](./report-cards).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a marking period?

A marking period is a grading unit inside a term. Every term has at least one. You can split a
term into several, for example two half-terms, in
[Grading setup → Levels](./grading-setup#levels).

### Can different levels have different term weights?

Yes. Each level has its own grading scheme. A primary level might use two equal terms, while
another level gives half of the year grade to a regional exam.

### Why doesn't a mark I entered change the average?

Check whether the column is published. Unpublished exam and free-assessment marks don't count
until you choose **Publish Results**. Also check that the assessment has **Counts toward average**
turned on.

### Why can't I edit a column?

The term is probably locked, finalized, or past its **Lock Date**. See
[When grading is closed](#when-grading-is-closed).
