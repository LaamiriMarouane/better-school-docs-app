The **grading scheme** and **marking periods** together define how a student's year grade is
built for a level. The grading scheme sets how much each term and each external exam counts in
the year grade. Marking periods are the grading units inside each term where teachers record
assessments.

<Callout title="Who this is for">
Administrators configuring the grading structure for a level before teachers start entering marks.
</Callout>

## How the year grade is built

A subject's year grade is built in three steps:

1. Inside each marking period, the assessments are averaged using the grade categories (Exams,
   Assignments…). See [Gradebook](./gradebook).
2. Each term grade combines its marking periods, using the weights set on the **Marking Periods**
   tab.
3. The year grade combines the terms and any external exams, using the weights set on the
   **Grading Scheme** tab.

**Example:** the grading scheme is Term 1 = 25%, Term 2 = 25%, Regional exam = 50%. A student
gets 12 in Term 1, 14 in Term 2 and 10 in the regional exam. Their year grade is
12 × 25% + 14 × 25% + 10 × 50% = 3 + 3.5 + 5 = **11.5**.

If a term has only one marking period (the default), the term grade is simply that period's grade.

## Open the level's grading settings

Open **Assessments → Grading setup → Levels**, then pick the level (or track) in the picker at the
end of the tab bar. The level's settings are split into five tabs: **Marking Periods**,
**Grading Scheme**, **External Exams**, **Level config** and **Period Configuration**.

The **Copy from last year** button next to the picker brings last year's grading schemes and
external exams into the current year. Levels that are already set up are left as they are.

## Split terms into marking periods

Open the **Marking Periods** tab. Each row is one marking period, with its **Term**,
**Period name**, **Weight** and an optional **Pass gate**.

### Default state

Every term starts with **one marking period at 100%**. You see it listed even if you never split
the term. Teachers record assessments inside that single period.

### Split a term

To divide a term (for example into Midterm and End-of-term):

<Steps>

<Step>
### Add a period

Click **Add period**, choose the **Term**, and type a **Period name** (e.g. *Midterm*).
</Step>

<Step>
### Set the weights

Give each period of the term a **Weight**. The weights of a term's periods must total exactly
100% — for example 40% and 60%.
</Step>

<Step>
### Save

Click **Save**. Until every term totals 100%, a warning names the term to fix and **Save** stays
disabled.
</Step>

</Steps>

Each term is independent — you can split Term 1 into two periods while Term 2 stays as one.

<Callout title="You cannot delete the last period in a term">
Every term must keep at least one marking period. A period that already has assessments or grades
cannot be removed.
</Callout>

## Set up the grading scheme

Open the **Grading Scheme** tab and build the list of components that make up the year grade.

<Steps>

<Step>
### Add a component

Click **Add component**. In **Type**, choose **Term** or **External exam**, then pick the term or
exam in **Component**. An external exam must be defined first on the **External Exams** tab.
</Step>

<Step>
### Set the weights

Give each component a **Weight**. The **Total** under the table turns green when the weights
total exactly 100%.
</Step>

<Step>
### Save

Click **Save**. **Save** stays disabled until the total is exactly 100% and every row has a term
or exam chosen.
</Step>

</Steps>

## Pass gate

A **Pass gate** is an optional minimum score. You can set one on a marking period (on the
**Marking Periods** tab) or on a term or external exam (on the **Grading Scheme** tab).

If a student's result for that period, term or exam is below its pass gate, the subject counts
as failed for that student, even if their overall average is above the pass mark.

**Example:** the regional exam has a pass gate of 8. A student with 16 in each term and 7 in the
regional exam fails the subject, although their weighted average is 11.5.

## External exams

An **external exam** (regional or national) is a component of the grading scheme, next to the
terms. Define the exam first, then add it to the scheme.

### Define an external exam

Open the **External Exams** tab and click **Add exam**.

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Name** | e.g. *Regional exam*. |
| **Code** | Optional short code. |
| **Default max score** | Optional. The score new subjects are marked out of (e.g. 20). |

### Choose the covered subjects

On the exam's row, click **Subjects**. Select the subjects the exam covers, then set each
subject's **Coefficient** (1 by default) and the score it is marked out of. The coefficient is
used only for the exam's own average, never for the overall average.

### Add the exam to the scheme

On the **Grading Scheme** tab, add a component of type **External exam**, pick the exam and set
its weight. The total of all terms and external exams must still be exactly 100%.

### Enter scores

On the exam's row, click **Scores** to open the score sheet: one row per student, one column per
covered subject, with the student's exam average. Select students to mark them **Absent** in a
subject, then click **Save**.

## Level and period settings

The **Level config** tab lets one level use different grading settings from the year defaults.
The **Period Configuration** tab does the same for one marking period: pick the period, then
change only the fields you need.

| Field | What it changes |
| --- | --- |
| **Display scale** | The maximum grade (e.g. 20 or 100). |
| **Rounding precision** | How many decimals averages keep. |
| **Primary format** | How grades are shown by default. See [Grading scale](./grading-scale). |
| **Pass threshold** | The pass mark. |
| **Calculation Method** | **Category weighted** or **Total points**. |

Fields you don't change keep the level's value, which itself starts from the year defaults. The
**Level config** tab also has a **Class ranking** switch.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I set up Term 1 = 25%, Term 2 = 25%, Regional = 50%?

First define the regional exam on the **External Exams** tab with its covered subjects. Then, on
the **Grading Scheme** tab, add two **Term** components (Term 1 and Term 2 at 25% each) and one
**External exam** component for the regional exam at 50%, and save.

### What happens if I don't split a term?

Nothing changes. The single period covers 100% of the term, so the term grade equals the period
grade.

### Can I rename a marking period?

Yes. On the **Marking Periods** tab, edit the **Period name** and save. The name appears on the
gradebook column header and on the report card.

### If I add an external exam, do all subjects get a score?

No. You choose which subjects the exam covers. For a subject the exam doesn't cover, the year
grade comes from the terms only, in proportion to their weights.

### Can two levels share the same grading scheme?

No — each level has its own scheme. Set each level up separately, or use **Copy from last year**
to start from last year's schemes.
