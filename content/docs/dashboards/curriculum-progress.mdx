title: Curriculum progress
description: "See where each class is in its curriculum compared with the plan, find classes falling behind, and spot lost or unrecorded sessions."

**Curriculum progress** shows, for every class and subject, how far teachers have got in the
curriculum compared with where they should be by now. It helps you spot a class falling behind early
— while there is still time to catch up — rather than at the end of the term.

<Callout title="Who this is for">
Administrators and pedagogical directors (every class), and teachers (the classes they teach).
</Callout>

## Where the plan comes from

There is nothing extra to set up. The plan is built from what you already have:

- **The curriculum**: the chapters and lessons of the subject's course template, in order, with each
  lesson's **Duration (Weeks)**. See [Course templates](../teaching/courses).
- **The school calendar**: the teaching weeks of the year. Holidays are skipped.

Lessons are laid out one after the other across the teaching weeks; that gives, for every week, the
lesson a class should be on. Weeks left over at the end become revision weeks.

What was actually taught comes from the **Activity journal**: each time a teacher records the lesson
covered in a session, the class moves forward. See
[Log what was taught](../teaching/sessions-and-attendance#log-what-was-taught).

<Callout title="Progress depends on teachers recording their lessons">
If teachers don't record what they taught, a class shows as **Not logged** and its progress can't be
trusted. Encourage teachers to fill in the activity of every session.
</Callout>

## Open Curriculum progress

- **Administrators**: go to **Reports → Curriculum progress**.
- **Teachers**: go to **Academic → Curriculum progress**.

Progress is measured at the end of last week, for the academic year selected at the top of the
screen.

## What the statuses mean

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Ahead** | The class is further than the plan. |
| **On track** | The class is where the plan says, give or take one week. |
| **Watch** | Two weeks behind the plan. |
| **Behind** | Three weeks or more behind the plan. |
| **Not logged** | Too few sessions recorded to know where the class is. |
| **No curriculum** | The subject has no curriculum for this year, so there is nothing to measure against. |

## The tabs (administrators)

| Tab | What you'll find |
| --- | --- |
| **Overview** | The key figures — classes on track or ahead, behind, not logged, sessions lost (cancelled, or teacher absent) and sessions recorded. Then a **Classes × subjects** grid: each cell shows how many weeks ahead (+) or behind (−) the class is; click a cell to open it. **Needs attention** lists what to look at first, for example sessions lost, or a teacher who records few sessions. |
| **By teacher** | Each teacher with their subject, classes, average pace and the class furthest behind. This is not a ranking: it helps you see who may need support. Click a teacher to see their classes. |
| **Parallel classes** | The classes of the same level side by side, subject by subject, to see if one is drifting from the others. |
| **Class** | All the subjects of one class. The tick on each bar shows where the class should be. |
| **Class × subject** | One class in one subject, week by week: the lesson planned and the lesson taught, sessions held or lost, holidays and revision weeks. Use **Compare with parallel classes** to see the other classes of the level in the same subject. |

## For teachers

Teachers see two tabs: **My classes**, with each class and subject they teach and its status, and
**Class × subject**, the week-by-week view for one of them.

## Frequently asked questions

### A class shows "No curriculum". What should I do?

The subject has no course template with lessons for this year. Build it in **Course Templates** —
see [Course templates](../teaching/courses).

### A class shows "Not logged" but the teacher is teaching.

Lessons are only counted when they are recorded in the session's activity. Ask the teacher to record
the lesson covered in each session in the **Activity journal**.

### Is one week behind a problem?

No. One week either way is normal and shows as **On track**. **Watch** starts at two weeks behind,
**Behind** at three.
