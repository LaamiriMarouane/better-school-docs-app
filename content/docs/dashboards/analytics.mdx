**Analytics** turns the marks, absences and homework already in the app into a clear picture: which
levels are slipping, which subjects are struggling, which classes stand out, and which students need
help before the end of the term. There is nothing to fill in — it uses what teachers already enter.

<Callout title="Who this is for">
Administrators and pedagogical directors (the whole school), and teachers (their own classes).
</Callout>

## Open Analytics

- **The whole school**: go to **Reports → Analytics**.
- **One level, class or student**: open the level, the class or the student's profile, then the
  **Analytics** tab.
- **Teachers**: open **Analytics** in your menu.

At the top, choose the **Term**, and what to **Compare with**: the **Previous term**, or the
**Same level last year**. Every change shown on the page (the small green or red numbers) is measured
against that choice. On the school page you can also narrow to one **Level**, and to girls or boys
under **Students**.

## The key figures

| Figure | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Average** | The term average. Each subject counts with its coefficient, and inside a subject the grade categories apply — exactly as in the gradebook. |
| **Pass rate** | The share of students whose average is at the pass mark or above. |
| **At risk** | Students flagged by the early warning (see below). |
| **Attendance** | Hours attended compared with hours scheduled this term. |
| **Not graded yet** | Tests whose date has passed but that have no marks yet. They are left out of every average until they are marked. |

## Needs attention

A short list of findings, most urgent first: a subject that is falling, a class pulling away from the
others, a group of students newly at risk. Red means getting worse, amber means worth checking, and
green is good news.

## Early warning

The early warning lists the students who need follow-up this term. A student is flagged when several
warning signs add up:

- a very low average,
- below the pass mark in several subjects,
- a drop since last term,
- many hours absent,
- homework not handed in,
- homework marks far above test marks.

Each student gets a priority: **High**, **Medium**, or **Watch** (one mild sign only). Students who are
flagged for the first time this term are marked **New**. You can filter by priority or reason, and
search for a student.

### Change when a student is flagged

On **Reports → Analytics**, administrators can click **Early-warning thresholds** and set the school's own limits — for example the
average under which a student is flagged, or how many hours of absence count. Leave a field empty to
keep the default. The defaults flag about one student in ten.

## Overview and details

Under the early warning, switch between **Overview · charts** for the big picture and
**Details · tables** for every number. In the tables, click a column header to sort, and a row to open
that class.

On the school page you'll find:

| Panel | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Average by term** | Each level's average term after term. A falling line means that level is slipping. |
| **Pass rate by subject** | Each subject's pass rate now, next to the period you compare with. |
| **Grade bands** | How many students are very good, good, fairly good, pass, or below the pass mark. |
| **Year over year** | The same level in past years. These are different students each year, so read it as a trend. |
| **Class × subject** | Each class's average in each subject, with its change. |
| **Classes** | Every class with its average, pass rate, change, students at risk and attendance. |

## Level, class and student

| Tab | What you'll find |
| --- | --- |
| **Level → Analytics** | Tracks and classes side by side, subject by subject; grade bands by class; exams sat by several classes; the level compared with past years. |
| **Class → Analytics** | Each subject next to the average of the same track; assessment by assessment; the students who improved or declined most; students who do well at home but poorly in tests; a students × subjects grid. With **Compare**, pick two assessments or two terms to see who went up, who went down, and who crossed the pass mark. |
| **Student → Analytics** | The student's average term by term next to the class average; strengths and weaknesses by subject; every mark with the class mean; absences and missing homework term by term. |

<Callout title="Comparing classes on a shared exam">
When several classes sat the same exam, their papers may still differ. Use a gap between classes to
start a conversation, not to judge a class or a teacher.
</Callout>

## For teachers

Teachers see **My teaching**: each of their classes this term and last term, next to the other
classes of the same level in the same subject; the latest test in each class with its mean and the
number of absent students; and the students to follow.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are the figures empty?

Analytics needs marks. When students exist but no marks are entered yet, the page says so. Figures
appear once teachers start entering marks.

### The figures look out of date.

Analytics is updated automatically, and each page shows when it was last updated. Administrators
can force an update on **Reports → Analytics** with **Refresh now**; the page updates on its own when
it is done.

### Why doesn't the average match the gradebook?

Check that you are looking at the same term. Tests with no marks yet are left out of both; they are
counted under **Not graded yet**.
