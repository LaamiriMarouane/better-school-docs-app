Teachers grade students through a few kinds of work. All of them meet in the gradebook, which
builds each student's subject grades and overall average. This page explains each kind of work
and how a mark becomes part of a grade.

<Callout title="Who this is for">
Teachers, administrators, and support staff learning the assessment vocabulary.
</Callout>

## Kinds of graded work

| Concept | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Assignment** | Work set for a class, such as homework or a project. Students hand it in online or in class, and the teacher grades it with feedback. |
| **Quiz** | An online test built from a question bank. Students take it on screen and their answers are marked automatically. A quiz is given to a class as a quiz assignment. |
| **Exam template** | The blueprint of an exam for a subject: its name, length, maximum score and weight. |
| **Scheduled exam** | One sitting of an exam template for a class, on a date, in a room. |
| **Free assessment** | A mark the teacher enters directly in the gradebook, such as oral work, participation or a notebook check. |
| **External exam** | A regional or national exam that counts in the year grade next to the terms. |

## How marks become grades

<Steps>

<Step>
### Marks go into a category

Each counting mark belongs to a **category**, such as *Exams* or *Homework*. Each category has a
weight in the subject. An assignment or quiz counts only when **Counts in the final average** is
turned on.
</Step>

<Step>
### Categories make the period grade

Inside each marking period, the marks are averaged per category, then combined by the category
weights.
</Step>

<Step>
### Periods make the term grade, terms make the year grade

A term with several marking periods combines them by their weights. The year grade combines the
terms and any external exam, as set in the level's grading scheme.
</Step>

<Step>
### Subjects make the overall average

Each subject's grade is weighted by its coefficient to give the student's overall average. The
grading scale can also show a letter or a **Mention**.
</Step>

</Steps>

The [Gradebook](../../teaching/gradebook) shows all of this in one grid per class, and the averages
update as marks are entered.

## Nothing is visible until it is published

Students and families don't see a counting mark until it is published:

- **Exams**: the exam is marked as completed, graded, then its results are published.
- **Assignments and quizzes that count**: grades stay hidden until the teacher chooses
  **Publish grades**. Practice work that doesn't count shows each grade straight away.
- **Free assessments**: they start unpublished. Their marks count only after **Publish Results**.
- **Report cards**: a card is a **Draft** until it is published.

## Statuses you will see

| On | Statuses |
| --- | --- |
| An assignment (student view) | **Upcoming**, **Open**, **Overdue**, **Submitted**, **Graded** |
| A quiz or a question | **Draft** until published, then it can be assigned |
| A scheduled exam | **Scheduled**, then **Completed** once it has taken place. Its results are **Published** or **Not published**. |
| A term's grading | **Open**, **Locked**, **Finalized** |
| A report card | **Draft**, **Issued**, **Published**, **Needs update**, **Superseded** |

See each detailed page for what every status means.
